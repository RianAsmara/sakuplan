# ADR-006: Hybrid ledger model (transaction + entries)

## Status

Accepted

## Context

PRD §11 explicitly weighs a full double-entry chart-of-accounts ledger against a simpler event log, and picks a middle ground: "a hybrid double-entry-inspired ledger" — enough structure for reliable transfers/balances/auditability without the complexity of a full accounting system SakuPlan (a personal budgeting app, not an accounting product) doesn't need.

## Decision

Model a **transaction** as a business event (`financial_transactions`) and its **entries** (`transaction_entries`) as signed effects on individual accounts. Income = 1 credit entry; expense = 1 debit entry; transfer = 1 debit + 1 credit (balanced); adjustment = 1 entry with reason-derived direction; reversal = offsetting entries for every original entry. See [[Financial Ledger]] for the full model.

## Consequences

### Positive
- Transfers are naturally balanced and atomic (both entries in one transaction row's entry set).
- Account balances are always reconstructible by summing entries — no separate reconciliation logic needed between "the ledger" and "the balance."
- Simpler than a full chart of accounts — no need to model an "external" counterparty account for income/expense.

### Negative
- Not a true double-entry system — income/expense have only one entry each, not a balancing entry against an external/equity account, so the ledger cannot itself prove global debit=credit conservation across all accounts (only per-transfer).
- `BudgetAllocation` and `TransactionEntry` have no audit timestamps — by design (immutable child rows), but it means there's no way to see exactly when an entry was written independent of its parent transaction's `created_at`.

### Risks
- If SakuPlan later needs full accounting-grade guarantees (e.g. for tax/business use), this hybrid model would need a real migration to a chart-of-accounts design — explicitly out of scope per PRD §18 ("no full double-entry general ledger for businesses").
